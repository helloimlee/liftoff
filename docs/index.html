A CLAUDE SKILL · MIT · BUILT AT MAXQ

Bring your own generator.

Generators keep getting better. /design shipped in August and made a whole stage of this obsolete overnight. Liftoff is the part that doesn't change when the tools do: it decides what good means before anything is made, and whether it landed after.

$ npx skills add helloimlee/liftoff

Or clone the repo and run ./install.sh. No build step, nothing to configure.

THE GATE

It grades the work against what you said it should feel like.

Craft and feeling are separate verdicts and both have to pass. A build can be pixel-clean, token-perfect, fully accessible, and still be nothing at all. That's the failure nobody else checks for.

THE LOOP

One command. Two stops.

The target and the pick are the only two places where being wrong is expensive and fixing it is cheap. Everything else runs without asking you anything.

ECHO · RECON

Says back what it heard, reads everything

Repeats your brief with its own assumptions listed separately so you can strike the wrong ones. Then reads your AGENTS.md, live stylesheet, components, past lessons and any video you point at, all in parallel. Nothing written, nothing to approve.

TARGET · IDEA

Three feelings, and one sentence

What this should make someone feel, and the mechanism behind it. A mood is not an idea. If your description could apply to nine other projects, you have a style.

Stop 1. You confirm the target. Everything downstream is graded against it.

EXPLORE

Whatever generator you have

/design in Claude Code. Real boards on a real canvas when Figma is connected. A tournament when the argument matters more than the options. Skipped entirely when the direction is already settled.

SCORE

Ranked before you look

Every option graded against the target, with one line each on what it does to the peak moment. Six artboards is a menu. Six with a target and a score is a decision.

Stop 2. You pick. The scores inform, they never decide.

BUILD · GRADE

Then earn the pass

Builds the pick with your real tokens, then all six graders run at once and merge into one verdict. Craft, feeling, accessibility, AI tells, motion. Failures first, and any one of them can send it back.

WHAT'S NEW

Still being built, mostly out of mistakes.

Every version below exists because something went wrong first. Here's the honest log.

0.14.018 AUGLATEST

Said the quiet part: it has no taste

"Bring your own generator" was quietly assuming someone who knows what to bring. Worth stating plainly instead. Everything this knows about good was put there by a person, and without one it's an instrument pointed at nothing.

Added the failure mode too, because a tool that only describes its good day is marketing. You can run the whole loop, collect a PASS, and mistake the PASS for judgment rather than proof your judgment got applied consistently. It can't rescue a bad target. It'll march toward the wrong feeling and grade itself green the entire way.

0.13.018 AUG

Stopped pretending to be a generator

Losing a stage to /design in a single day was clarifying. Better generators keep arriving. Nobody is shipping a better answer to "what is this supposed to make someone feel," because that isn't a tooling problem.

So it routes exploring to whatever you've got. /design in the CLI, Figma when it's connected, a tournament when the argument matters more than the options. Then it scores what comes back against the target before you look, so you're choosing with reasons instead of taste.

One command, two stops, the target and the pick. Bring your own generator.

0.12.018 AUG

Gave a whole stage away to something better

Anthropic shipped /design in Claude Code. It reads your codebase, works out your visual style, and hands back editable artboards. It explores better than this ever did, so it now does the exploring.

Keeping a worse copy of something around out of pride is how tools get bloated. The stage is gone. /design has the job.

What stayed is the part that can tell you no. Generating six options and picking your favourite is a preference, not a verdict. Best of what I was just shown is not the same as good.

0.11.018 AUG

Says back what it heard before it does anything

Asking a clarifying question requires feeling unsure, and the costly mistakes never feel unsure. They feel like knowing. So it now repeats your brief back with every assumption it made listed separately, under a line, labelled as its own invention.

You strike through the wrong ones. Takes about twenty seconds and beats finding out an hour into the wrong build.

Also stopped letting the model that planned the work decide whether the work is good. Turns out we had a rule about that and were quietly breaking it one level up.

0.10.018 AUG

It remembers now

Every run used to start from nothing, cheerfully rediscovering last week's lesson like a golden retriever meeting the same stranger twice. Now it reads what previous runs learned before it does anything else.

It files decisions too, including the direction that lost and why. That's the half everyone skips, and it's the half that stops someone re-pitching a dead idea in six weeks with fresh enthusiasm.

A note only becomes a permanent rule after it's cost you something three times, or once expensively. Everything below that bar is allowed to be forgotten, which is the point. It counts. You decide.

0.9.017 AUG

Learned to watch video, and to read the house rules first

It could read stylesheets, markup, images and research notes. It could not watch a video, which got awkward once it grew opinions about motion. Grading how something moves while refusing to look at anything moving is a strange position to hold.

Now it watches. Captions only by default, because captions are free and frames are where the money goes. It reaches for frames when the motion itself is the point.

It also reads your AGENTS.md before applying any of its own defaults. Your house, your rules, and it should not need telling twice.

0.8.017 AUG

Agents learned to take turns

Six graders were politely queuing to read the same file one at a time, like it was a single-occupancy bathroom. They all read at once now, finish together, and hand back one verdict instead of six book reports.

Writing still happens one at a time, because two agents editing the same file don't error. They quietly produce something wrong that you find on Thursday. Fan out reads. Serialize writes.

0.7.017 AUG

Added a gate that asks if this is about anything

Turns out you can hit the emotional target, pass accessibility, use every token correctly, and still make something fundamentally about nothing. The tell is that you can't describe it without showing it.

So there's now a two-minute verbal gate before anything gets drawn. Say what it is in one sentence naming a mechanism, not a mood. "Dark and technical" is not an idea. It's a mood board wearing a sentence.

0.6.017 AUG

Read someone else's homework, kept four answers

Went through a well-regarded animation skill collection fully expecting to take all of it. Took four. Skipped six, wrote down why, and filed the reasons somewhere permanent so nobody re-pitches them in a month with fresh enthusiasm.

The bar for anything new is one sentence: what does this do that nothing already here does? Two skills claiming the same ground means neither gets reached for.

0.5.027 JUL

Learned that a clean build log and a working page are different things

Shipped two boards with a spotless audit. Every check passed. They were visibly broken, because the layout tool ships every container with an invisible white fill and never throws an error about it.

Evaluate now has to render the thing and actually look at it. When it can't, it says so out loud instead of passing on structure. A clean audit on a broken render doesn't build confidence. It manufactures it.

0.4.027 JUL

The install script that exists because a README lied

Three animation repos, nineteen skills, and not one of them where the documentation said it would be. One repo's own install command points at a completely different project. Cloning them the documented way installs precisely nothing, silently, forever.

Now a script reads each skill's real name out of its own frontmatter and puts it where it actually goes. Not glamorous. Saves an afternoon.

0.3.027 JUL

Gave it a front door

It could run the whole loop but had no idea what it was looking at. Now it classifies first, says the read out loud, and pulls in whichever helpers the job actually needs.

Also taught it to read real reference images before generating. A written description of taste produces a written-description-shaped design, which is a very polite way of saying forgettable.

Full detail in the changelog. Watch the repo for releases and GitHub will email you when something ships.

WHO THIS IS FOR

It has no taste. It holds you to yours.

Everything Liftoff knows about good came from a person. The three feelings. The idea sentence. The reference images, the real tokens, the lessons that earned their place by costing somebody an afternoon. Take the designer out and what's left is a very confident measuring instrument pointed at nothing.

That's the design, not an apology. A FAIL isn't the tool having an opinion. It's the tool holding you to yours, consistently, at 4pm on a Friday when you'd rather it didn't.

Which is why both stops belong to you and never move. Max Q is the moment the instruments report peak structural stress. They don't decide whether to throttle down. That's the pilot, and it always was.

the targetonly a person knows what this should feel like
the ideatelling an idea from a style is taste, not logic
the pickscores inform it, they never make it
the overrideknowing when flat is correct here

The failure worth naming: this can become a way to feel rigorous without being rigorous. Run the loop, get a PASS, treat the PASS as the judgment rather than as proof your own judgment got applied consistently. A target written by someone who doesn't know what they want produces confidently wrong work faster than no process at all. The loop can't rescue a bad target. It'll execute faithfully toward the wrong feeling and grade itself green the whole way.

So it raises the floor for a designer with a point of view, and raises nothing at all for someone without one. Generators made options cheap. That moved the scarce skill from making things to knowing what to approve, reject and change, which is the one part of this nobody has automated and nobody is close to.

HONEST ABOUT ITSELF

It chains other skills, and degrades instead of breaking.

You almost certainly don't have all of these, which is fine and expected. Run doctor.sh after installing and it tells you what you have, what you don't, and what each absence actually costs.

resonancewithout it · the gate gets weaker
impeccablewithout it · planning only, nothing gets built
design-deathmatchwithout it · one exploration, no tournament
accessibility-reviewwithout it · a11y goes unchecked
gsap · threejs · motionwithout them · no timeline motion or 3D
watchwithout it · video references can't be read
memory/without it · every run starts from zero

None of it is required. On its own, Liftoff is still a charter loop with an emotional target and an idea gate, which is most of the value. Missing pieces get skipped and named in the summary rather than quietly dropped, because a stage that silently didn't run reads exactly like a stage that passed.
