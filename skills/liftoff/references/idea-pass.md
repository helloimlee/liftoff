# The idea pass

A verbal gate that runs between charter and explore. Named for Michael Bierut, who is a writer
first and has kept a sketchbook since 1982, and whose work is almost always one generative idea
rather than an accumulation of good decisions.

## Why this exists

Liftoff already asks how the thing should feel. It never asked whether there is an idea.

Those are different questions and a design can pass one while failing the other. A surface can
hit its emotional target, survive an accessibility audit, use every token correctly, and still
be a style rather than an idea. The tell is that you cannot say what it is without showing it.

This gate is cheap, runs in about two minutes, and kills the most expensive failure in the loop:
building something competent that was never about anything.

## The gate

**Say it in one sentence, out loud, before anything gets drawn.**

The sentence has to name a mechanism, not a mood. Then it has to survive four tests.

### 1. The telephone test

Describe it to someone who cannot see it. If they can repeat it back and it still sounds like
a specific thing, there is an idea. If the description could apply to nine other projects,
there is a style.

- Style: "Dark, technical, precise, with a warm accent."
- Idea: "Max q is the moment of peak structural stress, not the fire. The whole thing is
  about load, so the graphic is a pressure field that compresses and releases."

The second one survives being retold. The first is a mood board with a sentence on it.

### 2. The one-idea test

Count the generative moves. One is a design. Three is an absence of one.

Bierut's Saks identity is a single drawing cut into 64 squares that shuffle into effectively
infinite variations. One move, enormous output. The discipline is not restraint for its own
sake; it is that a second idea always dilutes the first.

If the pitch needs "and also," cut until it does not.

### 3. The generator test

Does the idea produce the work, or does the designer decorate toward it?

A real idea keeps deciding things after you stop. It tells you what the empty state looks like,
what happens at the edges, what the thing does in a case nobody has drawn yet. If every new
screen requires fresh invention, the idea is a veneer.

Ask: what does this idea decide that I have not drawn yet? If the answer is nothing, it is
decoration.

### 4. The name test

Can it be named in two or three words that are not adjectives?

"The Assay." "Max Q." "Flight Instrument." Those are nouns and they carry a mechanism.
"Bold and modern," "premium feel," "clean and technical" are adjectives and they carry nothing.
If the only available name is a pair of adjectives, go back to test one.

## Sketch before tool

Bierut draws before he computes, and the reason is economic rather than romantic. A drawing is
cheap enough to throw away. Anything made in the real tool acquires sunk cost within about
twenty minutes and starts defending itself.

So: rough the idea in the lowest-fidelity form available before opening the build. Boxes and
words is enough. The point is to reach the bad version fast, while it still costs nothing.

## Say it as you would present it

Write the two sentences you would open a presentation with. Not a description of the work, the
argument for it.

This is a gate, not a flourish. Work that cannot be narrated is usually work that has not been
decided. If the two sentences come out as a list of features, the idea is not resolved yet, and
that is worth knowing before the build rather than during the review.

## Failing the gate

Failing is normal and it is the cheapest possible failure. The move is to go back to the
charter's emotional target and ask what mechanism in the world produces that feeling, then name
that mechanism. Ideas usually come from outside the design: a physical process, a document type,
an instrument, a ritual. Assay, telemetry, pressure, certificate. Not from a moodboard.

## What this gate is not

Not a replacement for resonance, which decides what the work should make someone feel. Not a
replacement for impeccable, which decides whether the execution holds. It sits between them and
answers the only question those two cannot: is this about anything.

## Skip it when

The ask is genuinely mechanical. A contrast fix, a breakpoint, a typo. Demanding a generative
idea for a padding change is ceremony, and ceremony teaches people to stop invoking the loop.
